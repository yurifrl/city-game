# City genesis delivery plan

Deliver a deeply simulated, cooperative browser city through well-organized vertical slices. Code clarity, ownership, and correctness outrank feature count; playable progress comes from smaller slices, not shortcuts.

## Context
The repository began empty. This is a future implementation plan, not evidence of implemented features. [GENESIS.md](../../GENESIS.md) defines the feature inventory; [architecture](../../docs/architecture.md) defines boundaries and performance targets; [research](../../docs/research.md) records sources and tradeoffs. All implementation paths below are proposed and created only when needed.

## Steps

### 1. Author a building and place it

- Establish `crates/simulation`, `crates/server`, and `apps/web` with narrow world, construction, assets, renderer, and editor modules. Use the Rust authoritative command path from the first placement; do not build a separate solo simulation.
- Deliver an orthographic grid, pan/zoom, selection, placement preview, rotation, collision/buildability checks, demolition, and explicit save/reload.
- Deliver the first integrated building designer: footprint, floors/height, roof, facade palette, entrance, approved gameplay archetype, and published asset revision. Reuse the game's asset compiler and renderer.
- Run an early representative rendering probe with varied building recipes, shared facade textures, selection, and camera motion. Establish the device/browser baseline before expanding the visual pipeline; do not make an unrelated benchmark project a prerequisite to placing one building.
- **Acceptance:** create a building without code, publish it, place multiple instances, reject overlap, reload the same placements and pinned asset revision. The skyline reads like the supplied visual references. Record timings and resource counts; no unmeasured engine superiority claims.

### 2. Shape and build the same world together

- Extend server sessions/persistence and web networking; add the terrain designer's paint, raise/lower, and flatten tools through bounded commands.
- Implement owner/editor permissions, presence, authoritative command ordering, idempotency, entity/chunk revision preconditions, reconnect, and clear conflict results. Source drafts are local; published revisions and placed entities are shared.
- Protect occupied terrain and building entrances; show invalid brush results rather than burying buildings. Persist accepted mutations and duplicate-command outcomes before reporting durable success.
- **Acceptance:** two clients edit disjoint regions concurrently; overlapping construction has one winner; a duplicate command applies once; stale terrain edits fail clearly; disconnect/reconnect and a server restart recover matching world state. An unauthorized edit is rejected.

### 3. Make infrastructure matter

- Add simulation modules for road access and separate electricity/water networks. Deliver a small road kit, power source, water source/pump, service connections, and diagnostic overlays.
- Distinguish connectivity from available capacity. Rebuild affected topology after edits rather than flood-filling the entire world every tick. Start with connected-component supply allocation, not fluid particles or electrical engineering.
- **Acceptance:** connected empty housing reports available services; disconnected housing does not. Connected demand can exceed supply and produce explained shortages. Removing a critical link changes only the relevant network's service result; restoring it restores service. Power and water accounting respect capacity and declared units.

### 4. Move in people with needs

- Add `simulation` modules for citizens and housing plus citizen/building inspectors. Create stable individual and household identities, explicit housing capacity, life stage, rest/water/shelter needs, simple activities, and migration reasons.
- Use supplied housing and road access as initial eligibility; explain that affordability/jobs arrive in the next slice. Preserve consequential citizen state across save/reload.
- **Acceptance:** eligible households move in; full or unsuitable housing rejects further occupancy. Select a resident and inspect home, household, activity, unmet needs, and causes. Cut water, observe need/well-being effects, restore it, and observe recovery. Running with no visible residents produces the same authoritative outcomes. This is a settlement sandbox, not yet the complete management loop.

### 5. Close the first fun city-sim loop

- Add economy and transport behavior: workplaces, stable employment, wages, household budget, housing costs, explicit external food supply and shop inventory, purchasing/consumption, construction and upkeep costs, and city revenue.
- Add cached routes/travel time and basic home/work/shop/rest routines. Demand comes from suitable, reachable, affordable unmet housing; no arbitrary population timer. Keep traffic lane simulation out of this slice.
- **Acceptance:** players can grow a small solvent neighborhood by balancing housing, access, water, power, jobs, and food. A disconnected workplace harms attendance/income; unaffordable housing or missing food produces explained consequences. Spending and inventories cannot duplicate or go negative accidentally. Two players can complete this loop together, save, leave, and resume. This is the first complete management game.

### 6. Expand the creative engine without fragmenting it

- Extend existing asset/editor modules with setbacks, modular roofs, facade families, reproducible variants, terrain presets, shoreline tools, zoom-based LOD, and asset-cost reports. Add glTF/GLB interchange and KTX2 textures when the content pipeline benefits.
- Pin immutable compiled revisions; publishing an update does not rewrite placed buildings. Explicit upgrades validate changed footprints, occupants, costs, and network attachment.
- **Acceptance:** create a varied neighborhood and terrain through the designers, place it in a shared world without hand-editing data, and retain old placed revisions after source edits. Invalid or over-budget assets fail publication. Compare draw calls, memory, frame times, and visual fidelity against the recorded baseline.

### 7. Deepen the simulation one causal loop at a time

- Deliver individually: (a) sewage/pollution/health; (b) education/skills/job access; (c) goods production/freight/shortages; (d) preferences/leisure/relationships/memories; (e) aging/household change/migration; (f) traffic capacity/transit and richer utility bottlenecks. Reorder these sub-slices only when play evidence identifies a more valuable interaction.
- Keep each system in its domain module, with explicit inputs/outputs, bounded update work, and player-facing explanations. Add city services such as waste or fire only with their own useful causal loop.
- **Acceptance for each sub-slice:** a player can cause, inspect, and resolve a new multi-system consequence; individuals retain identity and consequential history; save/reload and two-client behavior remain correct; timing stays within the current workload budget. Do not announce Dwarf Fortress feature parity.

### 8. Raise scale from measured bottlenecks

- Maintain representative workloads alongside the relevant renderer/simulation modules. Measure dense repeated and heterogeneous buildings, moving citizens, editing churn, several zooms, concurrent editors, save/recovery, and network backpressure.
- Grow from a few thousand residents toward the documented 100,000-resident research workload; separately measure visible instances and concurrent editors. Introduce chunk streaming, stronger batching/LOD, bounded work queues, and targeted parallel computation only where profiles justify them.
- Consider Rust/Wasm kernels or custom GPU work only after demonstrating a specific bottleneck and an end-to-end improvement, including transfer/integration costs.
- **Acceptance:** publish reproducible workload/device/build results against the architecture's proposed gates. No hidden simulation degradation, lost accepted commands, duplicate effects, or world divergence. Revise capacity claims to match measurements; do not quietly reduce workloads to get a pass.

## Verification

Every step includes its own observable acceptance. Use real browser workflows, at least two clients for cooperative behavior, domain regressions for invariants, save/restart recovery, and measured performance. Current deliverable verification is documentation coverage and source grounding only: no game or performance benchmark has been implemented or run.

## Out of scope for the initial loop

Custom general-purpose engine/renderer, unrestricted asset scripting, client lockstep, peer-to-peer authority, MMO sharding, competitive economies, photorealism, full interiors, fluid-particle simulation, complete traffic AI, and immediate Dwarf Fortress feature parity.
